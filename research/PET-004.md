# PET-004 — Finite-flow-jet emergence prediction for mixed spectra

2026-11-24T02:14:10Z. Status: DERIVED, internal proof only; novelty and independent proof audit outstanding. No numerical experiment in this block. Extends PET-003 without assuming convergent mixed-spectrum normal form.

## PET-004A: uniform finite-information impossibility (COUNTEREXAMPLE)

Fix eigenvalues -1, lambda>0 and gamma(eps)=(eps,0). For any integer k>=2 consider
\[
x'=-x,\quad y'=\lambda y+c x^k.
\]
Exact solution: \(x=\epsilon e^{-t}\), \(y=c\epsilon^k(e^{\lambda t}-e^{-kt})/(\lambda+k)\). For c nonzero, first fixed Euclidean radius rho exit satisfies
\[
\tau_\rho=\frac{k}{\lambda}L+\frac1\lambda\ln\frac{\rho(\lambda+k)}{|c|}+o(1),\quad L=\ln(1/\epsilon).
\]
For c=0 the curve never exits for small epsilon. Stable manifold is \(y=-cx^k/(\lambda+k)\), so transverse contact order is k for c nonzero, infinity for c=0. Given any finite jet degree J choose k>J: same J-jet of vector field and same entire initial curve, different exit existence. Comparing c nonzero monomials of degrees k and k+1 gives two escaping systems with identical J-jets and different leading timing. Even fixed linear spectrum and fixed initialization order do not bound required information. Similarly, fixed linear field and initial curves (eps,0) vs (eps,eps^k) disprove uniform initial-jet sufficiency. These statements concern information from jets, not an algorithm with full symbolic knowledge of the analytic functions.

## PET-004B: adaptive finite-flow-jet theorem (DERIVED)

### Assumptions and statement

Let \(F(x)=Ax+O(\|x\|^2)\) be real analytic near 0, A real diagonalizable, hyperbolic with at least one positive eigenvalue and possibly negative ones. Fixed finite dimension, fixed analytic initial curve gamma(eps) with gamma(0)=0. Work in eigenbasis and Euclidean norm there for estimates; any fixed norm defines equivalent local first exit. Choose sufficiently small fixed physical radius rho. Denote largest eigenvalue lambda_max>0, smallest positive eigenvalue lambda_+>0.

Write formal parameter jets of the flow
\[
P_K(t,\epsilon)=\sum_{k=1}^K\epsilon^k X_k(t),\qquad
X_k'=AX_k+[\epsilon^k] f\!\left(\sum_{j<k}\epsilon^j X_j\right),
\quad X_k(0)=[\epsilon^k]\gamma.
\]
Each X_k is a vector exponential polynomial \(\sum_{\beta,d}v_{k,\beta,d}t^d e^{\beta t}\), with real beta, integer d>=0. Combine identical terms exactly and discard zero vector coefficients. For a nonempty list with positive beta define
\[
r=\max_{k,\beta,d}\beta/k>0,\qquad
h=\max_{\beta=kr}d/k.
\]
Both are computed from actual nonzero terms, not graph reachability. If
\[
(K+1)r>\lambda_{\max},
\]
then
\[
\tau_\rho=\frac{L-h\ln L}{r}+O(1).
\]
The criterion and coefficients use only K-jets of F and gamma (exact coefficients/resonance decisions). The first-exit law holds for all sufficiently small fixed radii (constants may depend on system/curve/radius/K). No claim of finite-precision robustness or polynomial complexity.

### Proof

1. Variation of constants for each linear forced coefficient equation yields finite exponential polynomials, because products of polynomial exponentials remain in that algebra and integration against each real eigendirection preserves the class, raising polynomial degree at resonance. Only lower-order coefficients enter f at each k. This computation is formal at epsilon=0, not integration of the full nonlinear trajectory; the coefficient equations are global for finite t even if an actual trajectory exits later.

2. Set \(t_c=(L-h\ln L)/r\), \(q(t)=\epsilon L^h e^{rt}\). On \(0\le t\le t_c-S\), for fixed sufficiently large S and large L, each term of P is bounded in norm by a constant times q(t)^k. For beta=kr, use \(d\le kh\) and \(t=O(L)\); for beta<kr, \(t^d e^{-(kr-\beta)t}\) is bounded on t>=0 and \(L^{kh}\ge1\). Thus \(\|P_K\|\le C q(t)\) when q<=1. For Taylor expansion of F through degree K, all residual powers of epsilon up to K vanish by construction; all higher finite products are bounded by \(C q^{K+1}\), as is the analytic Taylor remainder. Hence \( \|\dot P_K-F(P_K)\|\le C q^{K+1}\). Constants independent of small eps.

3. Choose mu with lambda_max<mu<(K+1)r. In a sufficiently small ball, using the diagonal eigenbasis, the one-sided growth bound of the difference of two solutions is at most mu (nonlinear Lipschitz constant <=mu-lambda_max). Bootstrap while actual and P stay in this ball. Since initial discrepancy is O(eps^(K+1)), Gronwall gives
\[
\|x(t)-P_K(t)\|\le C_0\epsilon^{K+1}e^{\mu t}
 + C_1\int_0^t e^{\mu(t-s)}q(s)^{K+1}ds
 \le C_2 q(t)^{K+1}.
\]
Here the integral is bounded by q(t)^(K+1)/((K+1)r-mu), and initial term by q(t)^(K+1). For S large, q(t)<=e^(-r S) guarantees the bootstrap and that no exit occurs before t_c-S (norm equivalence). This is the crucial control of the nonlinear remainder over a logarithmically long interval.

4. At t=t_c+s, with s fixed, substitution gives
\[
\epsilon^k t^d e^{\beta t}
\sim r^{-d} e^{(\beta/r-k)L} L^{d-\beta h/r} e^{\beta s}.
\]
Only beta=kr and d=kh survive. Let
\[
Q(s)=\sum_{k:\,v_{k,kr,kh}\ne0} r^{-kh}v_{k,kr,kh} e^{kr s}
\]
(omit noninteger kh). P(t_c+s) -> Q(s) uniformly for bounded s; this is a truncated asymptotic profile, not the exact physical limiting exit profile. Let k0 be the smallest selected order. Its nonzero coefficient vector lies in the positive eigenspace of A at eigenvalue k0*r: at that order and exponential rate the nonlinear forcing's polynomial degree kh cannot occur, since a product with total beta=kr must have each factor beta_j=j*r, and total degree kh requires each factor d_j=j*h, impossible at lower orders by minimality of k0. The derivative of a polynomial of degree d lowers degree, and the degree d+1 coefficient at beta=kr is absent by definition of h. Comparing highest-degree terms in the coefficient ODE gives (k0*r I-A)v=0. This proves a genuinely unstable transverse component; it is not merely a large stable transient.

5. Flatten the local stable manifold written as \(u=g(s_{\rm st})=O(\|s_{\rm st}\|^2)\) over the stable eigenspace; define transverse variable \(w=u-g(s_{\rm st})\). Its dynamics are \(w'=A_u w+B(x)w\), \(\|B(x)\|=O(\|x\|)\), by invariance and differentiability. In eigenbasis, \(d\|w\|/dt\ge(\lambda_+/2)\|w\|\) for nonzero w while inside a sufficiently small ball. At t_c-S for fixed large S, Q(-S) has leading term proportional to a nonzero unstable eigenvector of size e^(-k0*r*S); higher selected orders, graph correction and actual-jet error O(e^(-(K+1)*r*S)) are smaller relative to it by choosing S large. Then for small eps, \(\|w(t_c-S)\|\ge c_S>0\) independent of eps. Exponential transverse growth forces exit from the fixed rho ball within an additional bounded time (since w is bounded on that ball). Combine with step 3. QED.

### PET-004C: contact-dependent sufficiency and adaptive stopping (DERIVED)

Let \(p=\operatorname{ord}_{\epsilon=0}(\gamma_u-g(\gamma_s))\) be transverse contact order with stable manifold. Analyticity implies finite positive integer p unless the curve is entirely on the local stable manifold. If finite, flatten stable manifold as above: unstable Taylor coefficients through order p-1 vanish and at order p the unstable coefficient evolves exactly as \(e^{A_u t}w_p(0)\) with nonzero vector. In original coordinates u=w+g(s), the order-p coefficient of g(s) involves only lower-order stable coefficients (g has no constant or linear term). Those coefficients are sums of exponentials with strictly negative beta and polynomial time factors: prove recursively on stable restricted flow, including nonlinear terms and stable eigenvalues. Thus they cannot cancel positive homogeneous terms of w_p. At least one actual order-p flow term has beta>=lambda_+. Consequently at K>=p, r>=lambda_+/p. A sufficient degree is
\[
K=\left\lfloor P\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_+\right\rfloor
\]
for any known bound p<=P, since (K+1)r>lambda_max. This yields a finite-jet predictor despite arbitrary high-degree mixed resonances. Contrast PET-003 where ordinary initialization order bounds suffice; here transverse contact order is the needed quantity.

Adaptive exact-jet procedure: increment K, construct X_K and update r,h. Stop and report a=1/r, b=h/r as soon as r>0 and (K+1)r>lambda_max. Every escaping analytic curve eventually meets criterion (finite p). If entirely on stable manifold, actual solution follows stable restricted analytic dynamics and all Taylor coefficients have strictly negative exponential rates (or are zero); the procedure cannot terminate with an exit certificate. Exact stable-manifold membership is not uniformly decidable from finite jets by PET-004A. This is a one-sided finite-information certificate, not a decidability claim for arbitrary representations/computable real coefficients. For exact algebraic coefficients and eigenvalues the finite symbolic operations and equality tests can in principle be performed; cost may be large.

### Interpretations and scope

- No normal-form convergence, positive coefficients, or nonresonance assumption is used.
- Cancelling mechanisms are removed before maximization; resonance enters polynomial degrees.
- Initial seeds may decay; the eventual growing modes are identified by Taylor-flow terms not necessarily present in the initial state.
- The criterion is sufficient, not claimed optimal for individual systems. At equality the remainder estimate need not close. The number of formal terms and dense jet coefficients may explode; no total computational advantage shown. Near-resonances can produce severe conditioning and finite-epsilon crossover.
- The earliest asymptotically surviving coefficient gives a growing eigendirection near the onset, not a guarantee that it is the first measured coordinate to cross a fixed threshold.
- No constant exit-time correction or exact limiting exit profile proved here; Q is finite jet approximation with controlled error for sufficiently negative shift. The full flow and exit geometry can affect the O(1) term. Do not silently promote Q to exact profile.
- No semantic AI capability theorem. No claim of original theorem relative to literature.

## Discriminating exact check (symbolic, not numerical)

PET-004A with k=3, lambda=4, c=1 has X_1=(e^-t,0), X_2=0, X_3=(0,(e^(4t)-e^(-3t))/7). r=4/3, h=0 at K=3, (K+1)r=16/3>4; predicts tau=(3/4)L+O(1). Stable manifold initialization y0=-eps^3/7 cancels the positive term, leaving only negative exponents; no certificate, as required. For mixed spectrum with an independent stable coordinate and PET-003C's resonant unstable cascade, K=3 gives r=1, h=2/3 and 4>3, recovering L-(2/3)log L. These are algebraic checks; no new simulation claimed.
