# PET-011 — Full-state ReLU dynamical compression with out-of-sample certificates

2026-11-24T03:09:06Z. DERIVED internally; one controlled CPU check REPRODUCED. No established novelty or general AI capability result. Exact fixed-mask moment closure is known; the focus is the validity gate and uniform query error bound, and their measured cost versus direct microscopic replay. No claim of advantage versus existing optimized coarse-grained algorithms. Source-level literature comparison only, not independent theorem/novelty audit.

## Model and established closure baseline

Bias-free scalar-output two-layer ReLU f(x)=sum_j a_j ReLU(w_j^T x), x in R^d, N neurons. All a,w trainable by simultaneous Euclidean GD with step eta, squared loss (1/(2m)) sum_i (f(x_i)-y_i)^2 on m fixed training examples. No stochasticity, momentum or weight decay in this theorem. Initial full microscopic state given. Group neurons by their initial strict training activation masks sigma_g in {0,1}^m. Zero preactivation excluded by the certificate. v_j=(a_j,w_j) in R^(d+1). While training masks hold,
\[
r_g=\frac1m\sum_i \sigma_{gi}(f(x_i)-y_i)x_i,\qquad
M_g=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\eta r_g^T\\-\eta r_g&I_d\end{pmatrix},\quad v_j^+=M_gv_j.
\]
Let S_g=sum v_j v_j^T, P_g initially I with P_g^+=M_g P_g. Then S_g^+=M_g S_g M_g^T and v_j=P_g v_j(0) until switches. Training output f(x_i)=sum_g sigma_gi e_0^T S_g ell_i, ell_i=(0,x_i). This is standard moment/Gram closure on a fixed activation pattern. Wang et al., arXiv:2610.36180, describe exact coarse-graining for fixed ReLU patterns (five moments in their example); theorem-level comparison still required. Do not count closure itself as originality. The proof below is independent of that preprint.

## PET-011A — A full-state certificate for closure and future unseen queries (DERIVED)

For each initial group compute n_g, a center c_g (e.g. mean), enclosing radius R_g=max_j||v_j(0)-c_g||_2 and S_g(0). Store these plus P_g and mask; microscopic states can be discarded for this restricted certificate. For any query x (not necessarily among training examples) and each group define ell=(0,x),
\[
b_g(x)=\ell^TP_g c_g,\quad
u_g(x)=R_g\|P_g^T\ell\|_2,\quad
m_g(x)=|b_g(x)|-u_g(x).
\]
All actual preactivations in group are in [b_g-u_g,b_g+u_g] by Cauchy–Schwarz. A training step is certified iff for all g,i, (2sigma_gi-1)b_g(x_i)>u_g(x_i). By induction the reduced state and P are exact at that step, and the update computes the actual microscopic GD result. At first uncertified step abstain, do NOT assume closure remains valid; the test is sufficient, not necessary. Check all steps up to the horizon (at least all pre-update states). If finite precision, a validated error bound or interval arithmetic is required for strictly rigorous numerical sign decisions; this experiment uses ordinary float64 and large training margins, not machine-certified rounding bounds.

After successfully reaching a horizon, define proposed query activation from the center sign \(\hat\sigma_g(x)=1\{b_g(x)\ge0\}\). Predicted output
\[
\hat f(x)=\sum_g\hat\sigma_g(x)e_0^T S_g \ell.
\]
For any x, even when individual held-out masks split inside a training-mask group, we have the uniform pointwise guarantee
\[
|f(x)-\hat f(x)|\le E(x)=
\sum_g \sqrt{n_g(S_g)_{00}}\,[u_g(x)-|b_g(x)|]_+.
\]
Proof: for j in group, z_j=ell^T P_g v_j(0). If center chooses active, difference ReLU(z_j)-z_j=(-z_j)_+ bounded by [u_g-b_g]_+; if inactive, ReLU(z_j) bounded by [u_g+b_g]_+. Both equal bound [u_g-|b_g|]_+. Multiply by |a_j| and sum; sum|a_j|<=sqrt(n_g sum a_j^2)=sqrt(n_g(S_g)00). Sum over groups. When |b_g|>=u_g for all groups, E=0 and prediction exact (zero preactivations harmless for evaluation). For the training gate strict inequality is used. The exact-query set is the intersection over groups of union of two homogeneous cone constraints b_g>=u_g or -b_g>=u_g, each a second-order cone preimage. Hence the predictor specifies continuous unseen regions of the input space rather than a preenumerated set of held-out points. If no group ambiguity, the exact function is linear on each induced certified cone; one can bound linear relations over those cones by standard optimization, not claimed here as new. The E formula is an elementary but explicit extension to mixed held-out masks, not a claim of optimality.

For an operational threshold capability at a specified query x, initially f_0(x)<theta and finally f_T(x)>=theta, use lower/upper intervals [hat f-E,hat f+E] at initial and final. Certify acquisition if initial upper <theta and final lower >=theta; certify nonacquisition if initial lower >=theta OR final upper <theta; otherwise abstain. Relative to all query directions, this is endpoint threshold-crossing, not first-passage, semantic grammar discovery or accuracy against unseen target labels. The target is an evaluator-defined output relation (threshold); general specified target interval or linear relation follows by triangle bounds. The function can evolve substantially despite a fixed training activation pattern (both layers update). Does not show how to predict new training activation patterns.

State storage, counting dense matrices P,S, center/radius/count and m-entry mask per group, G active groups: O(G(d^2+m)), compared with O(Nd) microscopic weights. Construction from full state at least O(Nmd+Nd^2) arithmetic for masks and moments plus grouping overhead, O(Nd) input read unavoidable. Dense moment update can exploit M=I+rank-two modification for O(d^2) per group; dense generic matrix multiplies O(d^3). Sign checking and train output O(Gmd^2) naive (can batch). Query O(Gd^2) per x; direct O(Nd). Total conditional advantage in a regime N>>Gd, fixed d,m,G and long enough horizon/query volume, assuming no failed gate and comparable accuracy. This does not include exact bit complexity and any fallback reconstruction; if state discarded, cannot restart after failed gate without original microscopic checkpoint. Masks can have G up to min(N,2^m), hull/ball may be broad and gate may fail immediately. Cannot claim universal compression, worst-case sublinear input reading, or advantage against established exact closure for fixed masks. The advantage of added geometry is certified unseen queries and closure validity, not faster fixed-mask updates themselves.

## Decision and novelty boundary

Specific possible contribution: one-pass full-state compression with an explicit dynamically propagated mask gate and uniform mixed-held-out-mask error/abstention certificate, measured against direct replay. Originality not verified and result narrow. No claim of a foundational breakthrough. This closes a restricted full-state multi-example compression obligation, and reaches 100x state ratio but not 10x measured speed target in a favorable example; no three-class universality or held-out semantic task discovery.
