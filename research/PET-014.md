## Question and model

Remove PET-013's imposed exponential-small-seed/horizon relation. Study F(x)=x+g(x), g(x)=delta+x^2, delta>0, initial x0=-b, target b, 0<a<b<=1/8, delta<=a^2. This is unit-step GD for local potential L(x)=-delta*x-x^3/3, not a globally bounded-below supervised neural loss. The update is increasing on [-b,infinity), and x increases strictly at every update. Define tau=min{t>=0:x_t>=b}. No new semantic capability inferred; the scalar threshold is an operational transition.

The continuous-time drift model has passage clock integral dx/g and well-known leading scaling pi/sqrt(delta). Our narrower deliverable is a discrete-step certificate with explicit uniform clock error, tail work independent of plateau length at fixed a, and ordinary input encoding. Do not count classical inverse-square-root scaling as a research advance.

## PET-014A: corrected clock and uniform one-step defect (DERIVED)

Define
\[
H(x)=\int_0^x \frac{du}{g(u)}+\frac12\log g(x)
=\delta^{-1/2}\arctan(x/\sqrt\delta)+\frac12\log(\delta+x^2).
\]
H is strictly increasing for x>-1, since H'=(1+x)/g(x). For |x|<=a<=1/8, delta<=a^2, write G=g(x), u(t)=2xt+Gt^2. Then g(x+tG)=G(1+u(t)), and
\[
H(F(x))-H(x)-1=\int_0^1 \frac{x(1-2t)+G(t-t^2)}{1+u(t)}dt.
\]
Because |u(t)|<=2a+2a^2<1/2, and integral (1-2t)dt=0, bound the first term after subtracting 1 from (1+u)^-1 by |x|(2|x|+G) <=(2+a)G, using integral |1-2t|dt=1/2 and |1+u|^-1<=2. The second term <= G/3 in magnitude. Hence the convenient conservative bound
\[
|H(F(x))-H(x)-1|\le4g(x).
\]
Summing over any segment with pre-update states in [-a,a] gives error <=4(x_final-x_initial), because g(x_j)=x_{j+1}-x_j. No factor proportional to elapsed updates. A clock of this kind is closely related to standard backward-error/approximate Abel-coordinate ideas; not a new general method.

## PET-014B: forward/backward tails and integer crossing certificate (DERIVED)

Starting at -b, explicitly advance l steps until p=F^l(-b)>=-a. Then p<=F(-a)=-a+delta+a^2<=-a+2a^2<0 (unless exact equality at first entry, still negative). Starting from b, take r inverse steps on the positive branch until q<=a. Inverse is (-1+sqrt(1+4(y-delta)))/2. Monotonicity implies q>F^-1(a)>=a/2 for a<=1/8 and delta<=a^2, or non-strict inequality harmless. Set n=min{k:F^k(p)>=q}. Since F is strictly increasing on the full reachable region, F^r(q)=b, and F^(l+n-1)(-b)<q<=F^(l+n)(-b), applying F^r proves tau=l+n+r. This avoids mistaking a continuous crossing time for a discrete hitting step.

Let D=H(q)-H(p) and E=8a+8a^2. Before crossing q all central states lie in [-a,a]; crossing overshoot <=2a^2 by monotonicity. Summed clock defect <=4(2a+2a^2)=E for both n and n-1. Since H is increasing, n>=D-E and n-1<D+E. Consequently
\[
l+r+\lceil D-E\rceil\le\tau\le l+r+\lceil D+E\rceil.
\]
If both endpoints coincide, this certifies the exact integer step. For an interval enclosure [Dlo,Dhi], use the corresponding outward endpoints. If they differ, return the integer interval; exact step need not be identifiable at a given a or precision. This gives an exact above/below threshold endpoint decision at horizons outside the ambiguity interval. It does not certify arbitrary post-transition values or other coordinates.

Tail cost: for negative forward states r_j=-x_j>=a before crossing, the reciprocal increases by g(x_j)/(r_j*r_{j+1})>=1 as long as r_{j+1}>0; r_{l}>=a-2a^2. For positive backward iterates z=F^-1(y)>=a/2 during tail, reciprocal increase 1/z-1/y=(delta+z^2)/(zy)>=z/y>=1/(1+b+2a)>1/2. Hence l+r=O(1/a), uniformly in delta<=a^2. At fixed a, the explicit update count is independent of delta; continuous passage estimate diverges as delta^-1/2. Evaluating elementary functions with certified precision is additional work, not free. For fixed a and b and rational square delta, elementary function evaluation to fixed absolute clock tolerance via convergent series with guard bits scaling at least as log(1/delta) has sublinear arithmetic cost in the plateau length; exact tail comparisons and near-integer clock decisions can require additional precision or abstention. No general optimal bit complexity theorem. Ordinary rational x0=-b fixed and delta=1/M^2 uses O(log M) bits, not O(M). Do not extrapolate to general high-dimensional training.

For asymmetric passage, the correction can matter even asymptotically: take fixed negative start -b and threshold 0, apply same one-step bound with a=b until crossing 0. Then tau_0=integral_{-b}^0 du/g + (1/2)log(delta/(delta+b^2))+O(1), uniformly as delta→0. The omitted log term diverges; the bare continuous clock can miss an unbounded number of updates, although its leading relative error tends to zero. For symmetric endpoints the log correction cancels, and the bare continuous estimate may happen to round to the right integer (as in the verification). No claim of novelty over the established asymptotics of parabolic maps.

## Novelty and stop decision

Classical saddle-node scaling for maps, Dulac-time expansions, approximate Abel/Fatou coordinates, backward error and equation-free/projective integration already explain much of this construction. The explicit telescoping finite-error clock and exact integer certificate close the restricted ordinary-encoding obligation, but there is no verified theorem-level originality or realistic learning prediction. The 2026 Ghost Mechanism paper explicitly treats abrupt learning via saddle-node slowdown. The strongest existing baseline for this scalar example is an analytic iteration/validated passage method, not full step replay. No evidence of superiority over that baseline.

Decision PIVOT: park scalar temporal fast-forwarding. Do not make further large-horizon examples, repeat PET-008 or tune PET-012.
