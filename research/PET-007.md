# PET-007 — Loss-blind noncommutative learning dynamics

2026-11-24T02:30:08Z. DERIVED internally; exact finite example REPRODUCED. No verified novelty, independent proof certification, or inference-capability result for realistic AI. This is a controlled-training functional readout, not a demonstration of a network learning to compose previously untrained task primitives.

## Motivation and closest baselines

Static Hankel/minimal realization already recovers finite-rank weighted automata from suitable word observations, up to similarity; rank alone does not give a prospective capability theorem. Classical bilinear identification already establishes that constant inputs can be insufficient and richer pulse classes sufficient generically (Sontag, Wang, Megretski 2009). The specific question here is whether complete scalar loss telemetry, even with arbitrary curriculum interventions, identifies a noncommutative functional readout of strongly convex learning dynamics. The constructed obstruction is an exact special family, not a claim to supersede generic identification results.

## PET-007A — All-curriculum loss indistinguishability (DERIVED)

Let m>=4 be divisible by four. Use the real Clifford algebra Cl(m,0) as a real Euclidean vector space of dimension N=2^m, ordered monomials e_S indexed by subsets S orthonormal. Let Gamma_i be the matrix of left multiplication by e_i, where e_i^2=1 and e_i e_j=-e_j e_i for distinct indices. Each is a signed permutation involution and hence symmetric orthogonal, and {Gamma_i,Gamma_j}=2 delta_ij I. Write Omega=e_1...e_m (a basis vector, with Omega^2=1), b=(1+Omega)/sqrt(2), norm 1. If Gamma_S is the ordered product for subset S, then
\[
b^T\Gamma_S b=\begin{cases}1&S=\varnothing\text{ or }S=[m],\\0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Proof: left multiplication maps the two supported basis coordinates to S and its complement with signs. For S empty identity; full volume swaps the two with positive sign because m divisible by four. All others disjoint from support. In particular every vector-generator expectation is zero.

Two candidate models share b, known observation map y=b^T x, and labelled training objectives
\[
L_i^\sigma(x)=\frac12 x^T(2I-\Gamma_i^\sigma)x,\quad
\Gamma_i^+=\Gamma_i,\quad
\Gamma_i^-=\Gamma_i\ (i<m),\quad\Gamma_m^-=-\Gamma_m.
\]
Each individual Hessian has eigenvalues 1 and 3 (multiplicities equal), condition number 3. Nonnegative mixtures with weights summing to one have eigenvalues 2 +/- ||u||, in [1,3]. For a fixed mixture over duration tau, gradient flow propagator is
\[
A^\sigma=e^{-2\tau\sum_i u_i}\left[\cosh(\tau\|u\|)I+
\frac{\sinh(\tau\|u\|)}{\|u\|}\Gamma^\sigma(u)\right],
\quad \Gamma^\sigma(u)=\sum_i u_i\Gamma_i^\sigma,
\]
using continuous convention for zero u. Discrete GD update for a mixture and any real step eta is \((1-2\eta\sum u_i)I+\eta\Gamma^\sigma(u)\). Each belongs to the paravector space V=span{I,Gamma_i}.

For A=aI+Gamma(v), Q=cI+Gamma(w), direct use of anticommutation gives
\[
AQA=[c(a^2+\|v\|^2)+2a(v\cdot w)]I+
\Gamma\big((a^2-\|v\|^2)w+2(v\cdot w)v+2acv\big).
\]
This is a paravector with coefficients independent of orientation sigma. Thus repeated symmetric sandwich transformations by any sequence of stages preserve the paravector space and its coefficient evolution under either representation. For total propagator U=A_r...A_1, U^T Q U is obtained by nested sandwiches starting from A_r. Every primitive loss has Q=(2I-Gamma_i^sigma)/2; squared norm has Q=I. Initial state b evaluates any paravector to its scalar coefficient in either orientation. Hence all primitive loss measurements and state norms are identical at every point of any common piecewise-constant curriculum, irrespective of stage count or durations; weighted total loss also identical. Same proof for arbitrary discrete mixture GD steps. By continuity extends to bounded measurable controls on finite intervals approximated by piecewise-constant controls in L1; use continuous dependence of linear ODE solution. Any deterministic or randomized adaptive curriculum based solely on those same scalar measurements (couple randomness) receives identical transcripts. No experiment restricted to such loss telemetry can distinguish the models.

The statement does NOT assert indistinguishability given full gradient vectors, individual Hessian entries, hidden states, arbitrary input initializations, other readouts, or complete microscopic laws. With known full Gamma_i this ambiguity vanishes. The observational model is explicit.

## PET-007B — Sharp stage-depth barrier for one functional readout (DERIVED)

For y=b^T U b, expand U as product of paravectors, one per stage. If fewer than m stages are used, product has Clifford grade at most r<m (multiplying by a vector raises grade by at most one). Therefore no full-volume component, and b only evaluates the scalar coefficient. The two orientations (reflection in generator m) preserve that scalar coefficient. All such readouts coincide, even for arbitrary mixture directions and durations.

For exactly m stages \(A_j^\sigma=a_j I+\Gamma^\sigma(v_j)\), only selection of the vector term at every stage can contribute grade m. In chronological product U=A_m...A_1 the oriented volume coefficient equals det[v_m,...,v_1] (columns in order; m divisible by four implies reversing all columns has positive sign). Reflection of last coordinate changes sign; scalar coefficient unchanged. Therefore
\[
y_+-y_-=2\det[v_m,\ldots,v_1].
\]
If vectors linearly independent, exactly m stages distinguish models. More generally if the span of all mixture directions is a proper subspace, even an arbitrary number of stages cannot produce the top-grade component (Clifford subalgebra generated by that subspace has grade <= its dimension). This is an excitation-rank condition for this pair, not a general characterization of all learning systems. Pure gradient flow with generator i for duration tau>0 and sum u=1 gives a=e^(-2tau)cosh tau, v=e^(-2tau)sinh tau e_i. For chronological order 1,...,m:
\[
y_\pm=e^{-2m\tau}[(\cosh\tau)^m\pm(\sinh\tau)^m].
\]
Orientation sign is observable with m stages while all scalar losses still agree. Functional difference is exponentially small in m at fixed tau; no dimension-independent constant gap claimed. N=2^m in the explicit regular representation, so stage-depth lower bound is logarithmic in N, not linear/exponential in N. Do not interpret this as general NP-hardness.

Discrete example: eta=1/3 for a pure primitive gives A_i=(I+Gamma_i)/3, a valid gradient step with spectral values 0 and 2/3. Apply each primitive once in order. Then y_+=2/3^m, y_-=0; before training both y=1. For m=4 target scalar 2/81, tolerance 1/81: initially both fail, at the specified horizon + passes exactly and - fails by 2/81. These are actual scalar functional outputs from a previously untested curriculum, with the same fixed observation map and target; the target is engineered, not a semantic task learned independently. A predictor observing only primitive losses/norms cannot determine whether that output becomes functional, even if allowed all possible loss-only curriculum experiments. This is stronger than a finite time horizon obstruction in the specified observation class.

## PET-007C — Implications and positive diagnostic

For this model pair, an m-stage ordered readout with independent mixture directions is both sufficient and (in number of piecewise-constant stages for y) necessary. The determinant formula identifies which intervention directions carry orientation information; pure stages form a basis. Loss-only data identify no orientation even with unlimited stage depth, so changing observations is essential. If full labelled primitive maps are given, compute the product directly; if arbitrary ordered readouts are available, classical Hankel realization provides a general baseline for finite-dimensional bilinear/word models. We have not improved its generic sample/computation complexity.

The model is not a realistic language model; it demonstrates observational non-identifiability in a strongly convex quadratic learning family. It is a caution against inferring unseen future capabilities from training loss alone and suggests designing probes that preserve joint, ordered structure. Exact signs are constructed using standard Clifford algebra; originality of this specific learning formulation is unverified.

## Novelty boundary and decision

Closest literature: weighted automata Hankel rank and spectral realization; Sontag/Wang/Megretski input classes for bilinear identifiability (constant inputs insufficient); Clifford algebra and paravector sandwich identities; recent group-action and compositional representation identifiability. The exact separation between all-schedule loss indistinguishability and stage-depth-limited functional readouts under condition-number-3 quadratics is a candidate distinction, not proven novel. Review at theorem level before claims. We have not established predictive recovery of latent task algebras from full microscopic training data.

Next PET-008: return to actual learned functional relations and test whether early internal representation structure (rather than loss alone) can prospectively predict a held-out class of relations. Begin with one tightly scoped small algebraic learning system, no imposed factorization of the internal representation; predefine task/relation grammar, discovery and horizon rules, checkpoints, leak-free evaluation and strongest baseline (direct early outputs, loss, standard Fourier/representation diagnostics, and extrapolation). Seek an analytical certificate or a clear quantitative separation; if existing known diagnostics subsume it, pivot rather than polishing synthetic examples. No frontier models, paid compute, or sprawling benchmark suite.
