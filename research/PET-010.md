## PET-010A — Realizable bounded-noise indistinguishability with linear width

Use PET-009 balanced quadratic learner with input (x_1,z), z in R^2, training only on e_1, positive initial amplitudes v_i=A z_i, z_i=(i+1)/(n+1), i=0..n, A>0 fixed. Use FOUR neurons at each node (width 4(n+1)), each with a(0)=v_i, b(0)=sqrt(2)v_i. Write x=2As for normalized clock (not input), 0<=x<1. Define capacities c_i=binom(n,i)/2^n, sum c_i=1. For any unknown weights w_i in [-c_i,c_i], choose four frozen transverse weight vectors
\[
 u=\pm\sqrt{(c_i+w_i)/(4v_i)}(1,1),\qquad
 u=\pm\sqrt{(c_i-w_i)/(4v_i)}(1,-1).
\]
For each node sum u=0 and v_i sum uu^T=[[c_i,w_i],[w_i,c_i]]. Therefore every network in this box family has exactly identical training trajectory
\[
 F(x)=8\sum_i v_i^3/(1-xz_i)^3,
\]
identical physical clock dx/dt=2A(Y-F(x)), identical mixed input block C=0, identical transverse diagonal D_11=D_22=sum_i c_i/(1-xz_i), and sole unknown off-diagonal component
\[
 R_w(x)=D_{12}(x)=\sum_i w_i/(1-xz_i).
\]
Here D is quadratic output matrix on untrained transverse coordinates. R=(f(0,1,1)-f(0,1,-1))/4. Equivalently evaluate normalized directions and divide difference by 2. For all unit input vectors the absolute output difference from the w=0 network is at most |R_w(x)|, attained at equal transverse components. Training parameters and target fixed and public across candidate models; no gradients, NTK, full weights or arbitrary interventions observed. The clock can be assumed known, or recovered from exact common training curve; its acquisition cost is not included. Choose Y>F(x*) for any specified x*<1 so that all observation and target clocks correspond to finite common physical times.

Set w_i=(-1)^(n-i)c_i. The resolvent divided-difference identity yields
\[
 R_n(x)=\frac{n!(x/(n+1))^n}{2^n\prod_{i=0}^n(1-xz_i)}.
\]
Proof: the nth divided difference of v -> 1/(1-xv) at distinct equally spaced nodes equals x^n/prod(1-xz_i) by induction using the difference quotient. Multiply by n!/(n+1)^n to obtain forward difference, then normalize by 2^n. Positive and increasing for x>0. Two networks w and -w yield opposite future relations and identical training. If R_n(x_0)<=delta, every unit input observation at any x<=x_0 can be made identical by choosing the common w=0 output as observed value, with additive noise bounded by delta in each model. This holds for arbitrarily many observations and adaptive selection within the observation interval, under adversarial bounded errors. It is not a stochastic independent-noise lower bound. The future scalar absolute-error minimax risk is at least R_n(x*) by the triangle inequality, and a threshold halfway between zero and R_n(x*) yields opposite acquisition labels with initial relation zero. No full-microscopic-access impossibility. 

For x_0<2/3, an explicit bound is R_n(x_0)<= [x_0/(2(1-x_0))]^n/(1-x_0), using n!/(n+1)^n<=1 and each denominator>=1-x_0. At x_0=1/5 this is (5/4)8^-n. At fixed x*=0.9 the future gap also shrinks; do not claim constant gap there. For x_n*=1-2^-n, R_n(x_n*) -> 1: isolate last denominator 2^-n; product of remaining denominators at x=1 equals n!/(n+1)^n, so
\[
R_n(x_n*)=(1-2^{-n})^n\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1-z_i}{1-z_i+2^{-n}z_i}.
\]
The negative log is O(n log(n) 2^-n), since sum z_i/(1-z_i)=O(n log n). Thus arbitrarily small early noise can conceal an O(1) future absolute gap at sufficiently near-pole normalized horizon. Essential limitation: for fixed A, Y must grow at least on order 2^(3n) to reach this horizon (last node); large intermediate output scale and growing sensitivity. Not a uniform bounded-target, bounded-parameter-trajectory or fixed physical time constant-margin theorem. This near-pole example is ill-conditioned, not evidence for realistic AI scales.

## PET-010B — Exact minimax certificate for finite noisy checkpoints (ESTABLISHED optimal recovery machinery, model-specific application)

For fixed nodes and capacities known, observe y=Bw+e, |e_j|<=delta, B_ji=1/(1-x_j z_i), x_j<=x_0, forecast h^T w with h_i=1/(1-x* z_i). Parameter box |w_i|<=c_i; shared clock/diagonal known. The smallest worst-case absolute scalar error across ALL algorithms is
\[
 E_*=\max_{|w_i|\le c_i,\ \|Bw\|_\infty\le\delta} h^Tw
 =\min_{\alpha\in R^m}\left[\sum_i c_i|h_i-(B^T\alpha)_i|+\delta\|\alpha\|_1\right].
\]
Proof: at observation y=0 the symmetric feasible pair w,-w requires error >=|h^Tw| for any deterministic estimator. For randomized estimator the same lower bound holds for worst-case expected absolute error by averaging the pair. Linear prediction alpha^T y has error bounded by the displayed dual objective by triangle inequality, uniformly over the full box and noise. Equality between maximum and minimum is ordinary finite linear-program duality: dualize the inequalities +/-Bw<=delta, keep box support function, take alpha difference of nonnegative multipliers. Feasible bounded primal (zero feasible); strong LP duality. Thus optimal estimator is linear, a known optimal-recovery principle, not claimed novel. For given y one can instead compute conditional feasible min/max by LP, classify at threshold if entire interval on one side, abstain otherwise. Worst-case error of midpoint <=E_* by symmetry of the difference of any two feasible parameters divided by two; at y=0 equality. The formula includes all algorithms in this observational model but not observations beyond it.

Cost: m scalar observations, N_nodes known, dense LP of O(m+N_nodes) variables/constraints with O(m N_nodes) matrix entries; bit complexity and precision depend on encoding. Does not include discovering nodes/capacities or acquiring clock, actual measurement costs or direct gradient training comparison. Norm of alpha measures noise amplification and coefficient error sensitivity. At full parameter access no extrapolation is needed for this model (direct evaluation of grouped rational functions). No measured wall-clock advantage.
