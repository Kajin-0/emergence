## PET-013A: uniform-in-horizon skip-and-replay theorem

Let F(x)=Ax+g(x), F(0)=0 on closed norm ball of radius R, g continuously differentiable there (or on a neighborhood); induced matrix/operator norm. Assume Lambda>1, ||A||<=Lambda, integer p>=2 (real p>=2 works for these bounds), ||g(x)||<=b||x||^p, ||Dg(x)||<=c||x||^(p-1), b,c>=0. Fix rho>0 with 2rho<=R and
\[
b(2\rho)^{p-1}/[\Lambda(\Lambda^{p-1}-1)]\le\ln 2.
\]
For integer T>=0 and initial ||x0||<=rho Lambda^-T, true iterates x_j=F^j(x0) exist in the ball for 0<=j<=T. For 0<=k<=T define predictor
\[
\widehat x_T=F^k(A^{T-k}x_0).
\]
Then for k<T,
\[
\|x_T-\widehat x_T\|\le C\Lambda^{-(p-1)k},\quad
C=\frac{b(2\rho)^p}{\Lambda(\Lambda^{p-1}-1)}
\exp\left[\frac{c(2\rho)^{p-1}}{\Lambda(\Lambda^{p-1}-1)}\right].
\]
For k=T error zero. If b=0 the map is linear on ball, no nonlinear replay necessary. Otherwise k=min(T,max(0,ceil(log(C/eps)/((p-1)log Lambda)))) suffices for state tolerance eps>0.

Proof: bootstrap ||x_j||<=2rho Lambda^(j-T). With r_j=||x_j||, r_{j+1}<=Lambda r_j(1+(b/Lambda)r_j^(p-1)), so product from initial seed gives
r_j<=rho Lambda^(j-T) exp[(b/Lambda) sum_{i<j}r_i^(p-1)]<=2rho Lambda^(j-T)
since sum_{i=0}^{T-1} Lambda^((p-1)(i-T))<=1/(Lambda^(p-1)-1). Same envelope holds for hybrid trajectory starting at n=T-k with ||A^n x0||<=rho Lambda^-k. Convexity of norm ball justifies mean-value bound along segments. Telescoping nonlinear defect before n:
\[
\|x_n-A^n x_0\|\le
b(2\rho)^p\sum_{i<n}\Lambda^{n-1-i+p(i-T)}
\le\frac{b(2\rho)^p}{\Lambda(\Lambda^{p-1}-1)}\Lambda^{-pk}.
\]
For last k steps the Lipschitz factors for pairs inside the envelopes are at most Lambda+c(2rho)^(p-1)Lambda^((p-1)(j-T)). Their product <=Lambda^k exp[c(2rho)^(p-1)/(Lambda(Lambda^(p-1)-1))]. Multiply. QED. No sign or nonresonance condition, no convergent normal form required. Does not resolve slow-mode seeding at horizons beyond this sufficient fastest-rate envelope. No claim for arbitrary initial points or chaotic/global trajectories. If norm bound Lambda exceeds actual growth substantially, actual state may stay tiny and result not interesting; example has exact maximal eigenvalue Lambda.

For Lipschitz functional readout H on ball with constant L_H, output error <=L_H eps. Uniform family if a common Lipschitz bound is known. Certify endpoint threshold acquisition only when initial below threshold and final interval above; abstain if interval straddles threshold. This is a specified input-output relation, not discovery of semantic tasks.

## Computational and finite-precision interpretation

Dense n-dimensional linear powering uses O(n^3 log(T+1)) arithmetic, plus k map evaluations and Jacobian/majorant construction and output costs. If exploiting eigenstructure, use corresponding cheaper algorithm. The skipped nonlinear-update count is not a wall-clock ratio. For fixed Lambda>1 and constants, k=O(log(1/eps)), but if Lambda approaches 1 the bound can grow. No arbitrary-dimensional efficiency claim.

Crucial encoding restriction: as T grows, x0=Lambda^-T xi, ||xi||<=rho. For explicit rational x0 the input may already cost Omega(T) bits; the logarithmic-horizon statement is meaningful with a succinct scale/exponent plus mantissa (or preexisting exponent-aware state). Computing raw x0 may underflow even for modest T; do not claim ordinary floating point can store billion-step seed. Compute jump as
A^(T-k)x0=Lambda^-k (A/Lambda)^(T-k)xi.
Normalized variables z_j=Lambda^(T-j)x_j, so in the replay phase z_{j+1}=(A/Lambda)z_j+Lambda^(T-j-1)g(Lambda^(j-T)z_j), with z_T=x_T. For polynomial g, degree-l term multiplied by Lambda^(-1-(l-1)(T-j)), avoiding forming huge powers of Lambda. Norm of normalized linear operator <=1. For fixed dimension rational polynomial map and rational scale Lambda, approximate normalized matrix powers by rounded repeated squaring: each exact intermediate power has norm<=1; while operand errors<=1, multiplication error <=3 max operand error plus local rounding error. Depth O(log T), thus polynomial-in-T amplification bound, O(log T+log(1/eps)) fractional bits for the power portion, with fixed dimension constants. Replay normalized Lipschitz product is bounded by the exponential factor above, so rounding errors accumulate at most constant times their sum over k steps if neighborhoods maintained; allow a slightly enlarged domain/margin for computed states. This is a sketch of implementable precision control, not a general bit-complexity theorem for arbitrary analytic g or variable dimension. The concrete example below uses exact dyadic interval arithmetic and the explicit normalized spectrum, so no unproven numerical stability assumption is needed. If full rational input or exponentially many output tasks must be read/written, count that cost.

## PET-013B: multi-example gradient learner

\[
F(a,u,v)=\left(a+\eta u-\eta a(u^2+v^2),\,u+\eta a-\eta a^2u,\,v-\eta a^2v\right).
\]
A=[[1,1/2,0],[1/2,1,0],[0,0,1]], Lambda=3/2 in infinity norm. No claim of newly acquired semantic operation, and this is a deliberately exponentially small seed family rather than an empirical typical AI initialization. ## Literature, limits and decision

Chatziafratis/Roughgarden/Wang (COLT 2019) establish PSPACE-hard reachability of online gradient descent; Welper (2022) establishes bounded-memory Turing universality of constructed supervised GD networks. These already rule out many general efficient-prediction claims under complexity assumptions; do not infer an unconditional linear-time sequential lower bound or apply online result to arbitrary fixed batch. Classical Carleman iteration and Poincare/Schroeder rescaled iterates already provide close machinery. PET-013 proof is elementary finite-horizon telescoping, uniform local envelope and Lipschitz amplification; potential distinction is explicit truncation certificate for skip-and-replay on mixed spectra with succinct seed encoding, but theorem-level novelty not established. Rescaled Carleman powers considered and parked as unnecessary for this particular regime; avoid large tensor lifts when elementary linear warm-start plus nonlinear tail already suffices. No claim that near-equilibrium fast-forwarding itself is novel.
