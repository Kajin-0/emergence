## PET-005A: two-saddle finite-jet obstruction (COUNTEREXAMPLE)

For integer k>=3 and real c consider polynomial vector field
\[
x'=x(1-x),\qquad y'=(2x-1)y+cH_k'(x),\qquad H_k(x)=[x(1-x)]^k.
\]
Initial condition (eps,0), eps>0. Saddles S0=(0,0), S1=(1,0) have eigenvalues -1,+1 independently of c. Since H' vanishes to order k-1 at both endpoints, all vector-field jets of degree <=k-2 at both saddles agree for arbitrary c. The function I=x(1-x)y-cH_k(x) is conserved (differentiate using the ODE). Thus
\[
x(t)=\frac{\epsilon e^t}{1-\epsilon+\epsilon e^t},\qquad
y(t)=c\frac{H_k(x(t))-H_k(\epsilon)}{x(t)(1-x(t))}.
\]
Every c has a heteroclinic connection y=c[x(1-x)]^(k-1), 0<x<1, from S0 to S1. The connection topology, locations and linear spectra agree, not the entire geometric connecting orbit. The time to x=1/2 is L+ln(1-eps)=L+o(1), L=ln(1/eps).

For fixed downstream section x>=1-delta with 0<delta<1/2, choose |c| sufficiently small that |c|[x(1-x)]^(k-1)<rho/2 throughout 0<=x<=1 (rho>0 fixed). At section entry y is subthreshold for small eps. For c=0 the trajectory approaches S1 and never leaves. For c nonzero it eventually crosses |y|=rho on branch sign(y)=-sign(c), with time
\[
\tau_2=(k+1)L+\ln(\rho/|c|)+o(1).
\]
Proof: at t=(k+1)L+s, x->1, 1-x~eps^k exp(-s); graph term tends uniformly to zero on compact s, invariant term tends to -c exp(s). No earlier downstream crossing occurs outside a bounded window: invariant term magnitude |c|H(eps)/(x(1-x)) is monotone increasing for x>=1/2, and before the window it is arbitrarily small while graph term is bounded by rho/2. Within the window use uniform convergence and transverse limiting crossing. In fact on downstream section the signed graph and invariant terms oppose each other. Residence from intermediate section is kL+O(1).

Given any prescribed finite jet degree J, pick k>=max(3,J+2). c=0 vs c>0 differs in exit existence; c>0 vs c<0 differs in branch; comparing degrees k and k+1 with fixed nonzero small c differs in leading delay. All match the prescribed jets at both saddles and have the same heteroclinic connectivity. Even knowing the transition graph is insufficient. This is finite-jet indistinguishability, NOT indistinguishability given the full analytic germs (identity theorem). Smooth variants with perturbation supported strictly between local neighborhoods can preserve full local germs while altering global transfer: y'=(2x-1)y+g(x) with smooth compactly supported g; then (x(1-x)y)' with respect to x equals g(x). A nonzero integral over support seeds downstream y whereas zero forcing does not; this variant need not preserve the connecting orbit. No claim that topology or global transition maps are unknown in existing literature.

## PET-005B: conditional composition of scalar transfer asymptotics (DERIVED; classical overlap)

Assume a fixed finite itinerary of m stages, actual scalar positive entry variables z_j->0, transfer maps (already include connecting maps) with
\[
z_{j+1}=C_j z_j^{\alpha_j}(\ln(1/z_j))^{\beta_j}(1+o(1)),\quad C_j>0,\ \alpha_j>0,\ \beta_j\in R,
\]
and stage times
\[
T_j(z_j)=A_j\ln(1/z_j)+B_j\ln\ln(1/z_j)+O(1).
\]
All along the actual branch; transitions not counted in T_j must have uniformly bounded duration. Assume z_1=c_1 eps^{p_1} L^{q_1}(1+o(1)), p_1>0,c_1>0. Then for each finite j,
\[
z_j=c_j\epsilon^{p_j}L^{q_j}(1+o(1)),
\quad p_{j+1}=\alpha_jp_j,\quad q_{j+1}=\alpha_jq_j+\beta_j,
\quad c_{j+1}=C_j c_j^{\alpha_j}p_j^{\beta_j}.
\]
Proof: ln(1/z_j)=p_j L-q_j ln L-ln c_j+o(1), so ln(1/z_j)~p_jL. Substitute and induct. Total time through stage m:
\[
T_{\mathrm{total}}=L\sum_{j=1}^m A_jp_j+\ln L\sum_{j=1}^m(B_j-A_jq_j)+O(1).
\]
For given coefficients, O(m) scalar arithmetic to propagate, excluding construction of transfer laws and selection of itinerary. Does not establish efficient computation of maps, unknown branch discovery, uniformity for growing m, or new mathematics relative to Dulac/heteroclinic theory. This is an exact asymptotic bookkeeping lemma.

## PET-005C: precision and cancellation (DERIVED; elementary)

Log-coordinate transfer f_j(w)=-ln z_{j+1} with w=-ln z_j. Suppose f_j and approximation hat f_j are defined on a common certified domain, f_j is Lipschitz there with constant M_j, and sup |f_j-hat f_j|<=delta_j. Then E_{j+1}=|w_{j+1}-hat w_{j+1}|<=M_j E_j+delta_j, and
\[
E_{m+1}\le E_1\prod_{j=1}^mM_j+
\sum_{i=1}^m\delta_i\prod_{j=i+1}^m M_j.
\]
Use bootstrap to guarantee both trajectories remain inside domains and the same itinerary. If branch selector scores g_{j,b} are Lipschitz with constant G_j and approximated with uniform error eta_j, predicted top-two score margin >2(G_j E_j+eta_j) certifies same maximizing branch. If stage time is Lipschitz with constant H_j in log coordinates with approximation error xi_j, time error <=sum_j(H_j E_j+xi_j) including connecting times where applicable. Bounds require known local uniform constants, not just leading asymptotics. M_j=alpha_j for pure-power transfers and equality in aligned errors shows product growth can be sharp: alpha=2 repeated m times, initial error amplified 2^m. Preserving a fixed log-coordinate margin may require initial precision O(2^-m); no automatic scalable speedup.

Signed cancellation counterexample: two channels z_1=eps, z_2=eps+eps^k vs z_1=z_2=eps have identical individual leading asymptotic data (c,p,q)=(1,1,0). The connector z_2-z_1 seeds eps^k or zero, giving respectively kL/lambda+O(1) residence or no exit for downstream u'=lambda u. Changing plus to minus changes branch. For any finite truncation order, choose larger k. Thus leading exit certificates alone cannot be composed when downstream contact is controlled by cancellation. Need certified joint remainder and nonzero margin or higher-order correlated transfer information. This is a distinct precision barrier, not a new complexity lower bound.
